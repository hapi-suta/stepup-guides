## 1. Purpose

This policy sets the rules for how [ORGANIZATION] adopts, buys, builds, and uses
artificial intelligence (AI). It protects our people, our data, the people our
decisions affect, and the organization, while letting us capture the benefits of
AI responsibly.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all use of AI tools and systems by or on behalf of
[ORGANIZATION], including third-party AI services (for example chat assistants,
coding assistants, and document tools) and AI systems we build ourselves. It
applies to all staff, contractors, and vendors.

## 3. Definitions

- **AI tool:** any software that uses AI or machine learning to generate content,
  make or support decisions, or automate judgement.
- **Confidential data:** [DEFINE - e.g. customer personal data, health records,
  financial data, source code, secrets, unreleased materials].
- **High-risk use:** an AI use that makes or heavily influences a consequential
  decision about a person (money, health, freedom, employment, or access to a
  service), or that could cause significant harm if wrong.
- **Approved tool:** an AI tool on the maintained approved-tools list.

## 4. Policy statements

### 4.1 Employee use of generative AI
- You MAY use approved AI tools for [PERMITTED TASKS - e.g. drafting, summarizing
  public information, code assistance on non-sensitive repositories].
- You MUST review AI output before relying on or publishing it. AI can be wrong or
  fabricate facts.
- You MUST NOT present AI output as human work where disclosure is required.

### 4.2 Approved and prohibited tools
- You MUST use only tools on the approved-tools list. Requesting a new tool goes
  through [REQUEST PROCESS].
- Unlisted AI tools are prohibited for any work involving [ORGANIZATION] data
  until approved.

### 4.3 Confidential data
- You MUST NOT enter confidential data into any AI tool unless that specific tool
  is cleared for that specific data class on the approved-tools list.
- When in doubt, treat data as confidential and ask [POLICY OWNER ROLE].

### 4.4 Model procurement
- No AI system or vendor enters production without passing the AI project approval
  workflow and a vendor assessment (see the vendor questionnaire).
- Contracts MUST address customer-data usage, data residency, retention, and exit.

### 4.5 AI project approval
- Every new AI use case MUST be submitted on the use-case intake form, receive a
  risk classification, and pass the approval workflow before build or purchase.

### 4.6 Human oversight
- Every AI system that supports or makes decisions MUST have a documented
  human-oversight plan and a named human owner. High-risk uses require a human in
  the loop.

### 4.7 High-risk uses
- High-risk uses require a full impact assessment, human in the loop, an appeals
  path for affected people, [LEGAL/COMPLIANCE] review, and heightened monitoring.
- The following uses are prohibited at [ORGANIZATION]: [LIST PROHIBITED USES].

### 4.8 AI-generated content
- Content published externally that was materially AI-generated MUST be reviewed by
  a human and labeled where required by law or client contract.
- You MUST NOT use AI to impersonate a real person or fabricate sources or citations.

### 4.9 Records retention
- AI-related records (prompts, outputs, logs, decisions, approvals, impact
  assessments) are retained for [RETENTION PERIOD] and then securely deleted.
  Personal data is not kept longer than lawfully permitted.

### 4.10 Incident reporting
- Anyone who observes an AI incident (a harmful, biased, or leaked output, or a
  security event) MUST report it via [INCIDENT CHANNEL] within [TIMEFRAME].

### 4.11 Vendor management
- AI vendors are re-assessed [FREQUENCY] and after any material change (model
  version, terms, security incident). An exit path is maintained for each.

### 4.12 Monitoring
- Deployed AI systems MUST be monitored for performance, drift, fairness, safety,
  and cost, and the results reviewed by the system owner [FREQUENCY].
