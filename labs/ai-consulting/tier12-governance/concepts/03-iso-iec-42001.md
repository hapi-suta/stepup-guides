# Concepts 12.3: ISO/IEC 42001

**Tier 12 - Responsible AI and governance.** Teaching reference. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (published December 2023) is the world's first certifiable management-system standard for artificial intelligence - an "AI Management System" or **AIMS** (see: https://www.iso.org/standard/42001). Where NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework you self-apply, ISO/IEC 42001 is a standard an organization can be independently **audited and certified** against. Clients who want a badge to show partners, regulators, and their board pursue 42001. This module is theory only.

**Who this is for:** consultants advising organizations that want formal, certifiable AI governance, or that already hold ISO 27001 (information security) and want to add AI on the same footing.

**Why it matters:** ISO/IEC 42001 is becoming the "ISO 27001 of AI." If a client already lives in ISO-land, they will expect their AIMS to look and feel like their existing management systems. Knowing the structure means you can slot AI governance into machinery they already run.

---

## 1. What a management system is (and why the structure feels familiar)

A **management system** is a repeating cycle an organization runs to manage something (quality, security, and now AI) so it improves over time rather than depending on heroics. Every modern ISO management standard shares the same skeleton (called the "Harmonized Structure"), so ISO/IEC 42001 lines up with ISO 27001 and ISO 9001. If your client has one, the new one drops into the same slots.

The engine underneath is **Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA)**:
- **Plan:** understand context, set policy and objectives, assess risk.
- **Do:** implement controls and operate them.
- **Check:** measure performance, audit internally.
- **Act:** management reviews and drives continual improvement.

---

## 2. The clauses you must know

ISO/IEC 42001 is organized into numbered clauses. You do not memorize the text, but you must know what each clause requires so you can check a client against it. These map to the plan items in your spec.

### Organizational context (Clause 4)
Understand the organization, its purpose, its interested parties (customers, regulators, staff, the public), and the scope of the AIMS. Define what AI systems and activities are in scope. This is where the boundary of governance is drawn.

### Leadership (Clause 5)
Top management must own the AIMS: demonstrate commitment, set an AI policy, and assign roles and responsibilities. Governance without leadership sign-off fails - this clause forces accountability upward.

### Policy (Clause 5.2, under Leadership)
A documented AI policy stating the organization's commitments and principles (this is a deliverable in your toolkit). It must align with the organization's context and objectives and be communicated. Note the placement: the AI policy is **not** a standalone top-level clause - it is clause **5.2**, a sub-clause of Leadership, exactly as the information-security policy is 5.2 in ISO 27001. Knowing that mapping keeps you credible when a client cross-references the standard.

### Planning: risk and opportunity, AI objectives (Clause 6)
Identify risks and opportunities, and set measurable AI objectives with plans to reach them. Two distinct, numbered requirements live here and you should keep them straight: the **AI risk assessment** (clause 6.1.2) and the **AI system impact assessment** (clause 6.1.4). The impact-assessment requirement is one 42001 makes explicit and that ISO 27001 has no equivalent for - it is a defining feature of the standard (see: https://www.iso.org/standard/42001).

### Support (Clause 7)
The resources that make it work: competent people, awareness and training, communication, and **documented information** (document control). Auditors will ask for evidence, so documentation discipline lives here.

### Operational controls (Clause 8, plus Annex A / Annex B)
Operational planning and control across the AI lifecycle: data management, development, deployment, third-party and supplier relationships, and use. Annex A lists reference **controls** (like ISO 27001's Annex A) and Annex B gives implementation guidance. This is where responsible-AI principles turn into concrete operating procedures.

### Performance evaluation (Clause 9)
Monitor, measure, analyze, and evaluate the AIMS. Clause 9 has two sub-parts you must know by name:

- **Internal audit** (clause 9.2): a structured, independent check that the AIMS is working as designed and conforms to the standard. Findings feed the management review.
- **Management review** (clause 9.3): leadership formally reviews the AIMS at planned intervals - audit results, incidents, objectives, changes in risk - and decides on improvements and resources.

### Improvement (Clause 10) / continual improvement
Handle nonconformities and corrective actions, and continually improve the AIMS. The loop never closes; it spirals upward. This is the "Act" in PDCA.

## 3. NIST AI RMF vs ISO/IEC 42001 - the comparison clients ask for

| | NIST AI RMF | ISO/IEC 42001 |
|--|-------------|----------------|
| Type | Voluntary framework | Certifiable management-system standard |
| Certification | No (self-applied) | Yes (third-party audit) |
| Origin | US (NIST) | International (ISO/IEC) |
| Shape | Four functions (Govern/Map/Measure/Manage) | Clauses + Annex A controls (PDCA) |
| Best when | You want flexible, rights-focused risk management | You want an auditable badge and integration with ISO 27001/9001 |

They are complementary, not competing. A common consulting recommendation: **use NIST AI RMF to organize the risk thinking, and ISO/IEC 42001 to formalize and certify the management system.** Say exactly that in an interview.

## 4. Applying ISO/IEC 42001 as a consultant

You will rarely run a full certification yourself early on, but you must be able to:

1. **Gap-assess:** walk the clauses (context, leadership, policy, planning, support, operations, performance evaluation, improvement) and note what exists vs what is missing.
2. **Map artifacts to clauses:** your AI policy satisfies the policy clause; your impact-assessment template satisfies part of Clause 6; your inventory and vendor questionnaire satisfy Clause 8; your incident template and management dashboard feed Clause 9.
3. **Recommend a path:** if the client wants certification, lay out the sequence (scope, policy, risk and impact assessments, controls, internal audit, management review, external audit).

Your toolkit in this tier's BUILD is deliberately clause-aligned: each artifact you produce is evidence an auditor would ask for.

---

## Key takeaways

- ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the first certifiable AI management-system standard - an AIMS. NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework; 42001 you can be audited and certified against.
- It shares the ISO Harmonized Structure (PDCA), so it fits alongside ISO 27001 and 9001.
- Know the clause themes: context, leadership, policy, planning (risk + impact assessment + objectives), support (competence + documentation), operational controls (Annex A), performance evaluation (internal audit + management review), improvement (continual).
- The consulting line: use NIST AI RMF to organize risk thinking; use ISO/IEC 42001 to formalize and certify the management system. They complement each other.
- Every toolkit artifact you build maps to a clause an auditor will request.
- Clause numbers to keep straight: policy is 5.2 (under Leadership), AI risk assessment is 6.1.2, AI system impact assessment is 6.1.4, internal audit is 9.2, management review is 9.3.
