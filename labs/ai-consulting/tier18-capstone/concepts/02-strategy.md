# Concepts: Capstone Part 2 - Strategy

**Tier 18, Module 18.2** - turning discovery findings into a defensible plan.

Strategy converts what you learned in discovery into decisions: how ready they are, what to build first, whether it is worth it, how risky it is, and how you will know it worked. This module covers the six strategy outputs.

**The rule of strategy:** every recommendation traces back to a discovery finding. No new assertions appear here that discovery did not support.

---

## The six strategy outputs

### 1. AI readiness score
Score the institution across the readiness dimensions (data, governance, skills, technical, use-case, and the public-sector overlays of accessibility and appeals readiness). Use the Tier 16 readiness assessment method. The score is honest: a low score is a valuable finding, not a failure to hide.

Produce: a scored readiness assessment with a plain-language verdict and the top gaps to close first.

### 2. Use-case prioritization
Rank the candidate use cases from discovery by value, risk, and effort. For a public institution, sequence from administrative and internal (low risk, high value) toward citizen-facing (high risk) only once the foundation exists. The internal, staff-facing, cited document assistant is almost always the right Phase 1.

Produce: a prioritized use-case list with the chosen first use case and the rationale.

### 3. Business case
Justify the investment in the client's terms. For public sector that is usually staff time saved, service quality, consistency, and accessibility - not revenue. Be honest about costs, including the unglamorous ones (data classification, ongoing maintenance, inference cost).

Produce: a business case with costs, benefits, and the break-even logic.

### 4. Risk classification
Classify the chosen use case's risk using your governance framework (NIST AI RMF style, see: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). An internal cited assistant is lower-risk; anything touching a high-impact citizen decision is high-risk and triggers the full oversight and impact-assessment machinery.

Produce: a risk classification with the controls each level requires.

### 5. Pilot charter
Define the pilot precisely: the one use case, its scope and explicit non-scope, its timeline, its owner, and its guardrails. A charter the sponsor signs is what turns a nice idea into a funded project.

Produce: a signed pilot charter.

### 6. Success metrics
Define measurable success before you build, so nobody moves the goalposts later. For an internal assistant: search-time reduction, staff adoption, answer accuracy (per language), citation trust, and zero privacy or security incidents.

Produce: a success-metrics table with baselines, targets, and how each is measured.

---

## The strategy output: a decision-ready package

Roll the six outputs into a Strategy document that leadership can approve. Its job is to answer, in order: Are we ready? What do we build first? Is it worth it? How risky is it? What exactly is the pilot? How will we know it worked? If a leader can read it and say yes to a scoped, funded pilot, the strategy succeeded.

---

## Strategy rules (memorize)

1. **Every recommendation traces to a discovery finding.**
2. **Start administrative and internal; earn the way up.**
3. **Score readiness honestly** - a low score is a finding, not something to hide.
4. **Define success metrics before building** so goalposts cannot move.
5. **The pilot charter is the commitment device.** Get it signed.
