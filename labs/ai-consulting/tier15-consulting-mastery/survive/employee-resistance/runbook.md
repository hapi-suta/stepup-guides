# SURVIVE Runbook: Employee Resistance Stalls Adoption

**Tier 15 - SURVIVE scenario 3 of 3**

This is a review-assessed scenario. There is no script to run. You apply change management to recover a stalled pilot, and a reviewer judges whether your plan addresses the real cause of resistance rather than just pushing harder.

---

## The situation

The 90-day pilot you designed for [CLIENT] is live: an AI assistant that drafts case notes for social workers, freeing them from paperwork. The technology works. In testing, drafts were good and time-savings were real. But at day 40, adoption has stalled at around 20 percent and is falling, not rising.

When you dig in, you hear:
- "If this writes my notes, what am I here for? Is this how they replace us?"
- "Nobody asked us. IT just dropped it on us."
- "I do not trust it to get a sensitive case right, and it is my name on the record."
- "I do not have time to learn a new tool on top of my caseload."
- "My supervisor still measures me on cases closed, so why bother?"

The client sponsor's instinct is to mandate it: "Just make them use it." You know that mandates without addressing fear produce malicious compliance, not adoption. The pilot's success metric (reduced documentation time) will fail not because the tool is bad, but because people will not use it.

Your job: apply change management to recover the pilot, addressing the real cause of resistance, not just pushing harder.

---

## Why this happens

Adoption is a people problem far more often than a technology problem. The resistance here is textbook and has clear causes:

- **Fear for their jobs** (the loudest and most legitimate). "AI writes my notes" sounds like "AI replaces me."
- **No involvement.** The tool was imposed, not co-designed. People resist what is done TO them.
- **Trust and accountability.** Their name is on the record; they will not delegate a sensitive judgment to a tool they did not learn to trust.
- **No time and no training.** Learning a tool is extra work on top of a full caseload.
- **Misaligned incentives.** They are measured on the old metric, so the new behaviour is unrewarded.

A recognized model helps you be systematic. **ADKAR** (Awareness, Desire, Ability, Reinforcement) maps the resistance precisely, and it shows that mandating jumps straight to "do it" while skipping Awareness, Desire, and Ability - which is exactly why mandates fail.

---

## Diagnosis: map resistance to its cause with ADKAR

Do not treat "they won't use it" as one problem. Break it down:

| ADKAR stage | Question | This pilot's gap |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Do they know WHY this is happening? | No - they think it is a prelude to layoffs |
| Desire | Do they WANT to participate? | No - fear and no personal benefit felt |
| Ability | Can they actually use it? | Partly - no real training, no time |
| Knowledge | Do they know HOW? | Weak - dropped on them |
| Reinforcement | Is the new behaviour rewarded and sustained? | No - old metric still rules |

The biggest gaps are Awareness (job fear) and Desire (no felt benefit, no involvement). A mandate attacks the symptom while leaving both untouched, which is why the sponsor's instinct will backfire.

---

## Recovery: apply change management to save the pilot

### 1. Talk the sponsor out of the mandate (first, and privately)
"Mandating it will get us compliance on paper and sabotage underneath - people will run it and ignore the output, and our metric still fails. Give me two weeks to address the real cause, then we reassess." Protect the pilot from a well-meant but fatal move.

### 2. Build Awareness: name the job fear directly and honestly
Do not dodge the layoff question - address it head-on with the sponsor's authority behind you. "This tool exists to take documentation off your plate so you spend more time with clients, not to reduce headcount." If that is true, have leadership say it publicly and in writing. If job impact IS real, be honest about it and address it with retraining/redeployment - hiding it destroys all trust. Awareness is impossible without honesty here.

### 3. Build Desire: involve them and show personal benefit
- Bring a handful of respected social workers into a redesign session. Let them shape how it fits their workflow. People adopt what they helped build.
- Frame the benefit in THEIR terms: "an hour less paperwork a day, more time on cases," measured and shown.
- Recruit **champions** from among the respected skeptics-turned-believers. Peer endorsement beats top-down mandate every time.

### 4. Build Ability and Knowledge: real training and protected time
- Provide short, role-specific training, not a generic demo.
- Give protected time to learn - do not expect adoption on top of a full caseload with zero slack.
- Address the accountability fear: make clear the human always reviews and edits; the tool drafts, the worker owns. Show them how to check and correct it so they trust their name on the output.

### 5. Fix Reinforcement: align incentives and measurement
- Get supervisors to recognize and support tool use, and stop measuring people purely on the old metric that punishes time spent learning.
- Publish early wins from the champions ("[name] cut documentation time by X, more client time").
- Make feedback easy and act on it visibly, so users see the tool improving because of them.

### 6. Set a communication plan and re-baseline the metric
- A simple plan: who hears what, from whom, when (leadership on the why, supervisors on support, champions on peer proof).
- Because 40 days were lost, extend or re-baseline the pilot's measurement window so the recovery has a fair test, and re-confirm the go/no-go rule with the sponsor.

## What you must produce for this scenario

1. **An ADKAR diagnosis** mapping each resistance quote to its stage and gap.
2. **A note to the sponsor** arguing against the mandate and proposing the change-management recovery, including honesty about job impact.
3. **A change-management action plan** covering Awareness (job-fear communication), Desire (involvement + champions + felt benefit), Ability/Knowledge (training + protected time + accountability), and Reinforcement (aligned incentives + early wins + feedback).
4. **A stakeholder map** and a short communication plan (who says what, to whom, when).
5. **A re-baselined measurement plan** so the recovered pilot gets a fair go/no-go test.

## Decision checklist (self-assess or reviewer-assess)

- [ ] You diagnosed the resistance by cause (using ADKAR or equivalent), not as one undifferentiated "they won't use it."
- [ ] You talked the sponsor out of a bare mandate and explained why it backfires.
- [ ] You addressed the job-fear directly and honestly - not dodged, not falsely reassured.
- [ ] You built Desire through genuine involvement and peer champions, not top-down pressure.
- [ ] You provided real, role-specific training AND protected time to learn.
- [ ] You addressed the accountability fear (human reviews and owns the output).
- [ ] You aligned incentives/measurement so the new behaviour is rewarded, not punished.
- [ ] You re-baselined the metric so the recovery gets a fair test.
- [ ] Your plan treats adoption as a people problem, not a tooling or willpower problem.

If any box is unchecked, you are still pushing harder instead of removing the real blocker. Rework.

---

## What you learned

- Stalled adoption is almost always a people problem, not a technology problem. Diagnose the CAUSE (ADKAR) before acting.
- Mandates produce malicious compliance, not adoption. They skip Awareness, Desire, and Ability - the very things that are broken.
- Address job fear honestly. Honesty (even about real impact) builds the trust adoption needs; false reassurance destroys it.
- Recover adoption with involvement, champions, real training, protected time, and aligned incentives - then re-baseline the metric so the pilot gets a fair verdict.
